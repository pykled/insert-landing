# Venue website pattern survey — October 2026

**Method.** 36 live venue homepages were fetched with `curl -sL` (Chrome UA) on 2026-10-20, the HTML was grepped for platform assets, font declarations, `<video>`/iframe/form markup and capacity/price text, and each homepage was screenshotted at 1440px in headless Chrome. Every claim below comes from that HTML or screenshot. Where a detail could not be confirmed it is marked **not verified**. No venue was contacted. "Platform" is inferred from meta generator tags and asset hosts (Showit = `showit.co`, Squarespace = `squarespace.com`/`static1.squarespace.com`, Wix = `wixstatic`/generator meta, WordPress = `wp-content`, Webflow = `website-files.com`, Duda = `irp.cdn-website`/`dmRoot`, GoHighLevel = `msgsndr`/`leadconnector`).

Sample mix: 24 Southern California venues (Malibu, Simi Valley, Agoura, Moorpark, Pala, Pioneertown, Temecula, Santa Ynez, Goleta, San Juan Capistrano, Silverado, Fallbrook, DTLA, Culver City, Westlake, Hollywood, La Jolla, Coronado, Palm Springs, Desert Hot Springs, Wilshire) and 12 nationally cited venues (NY, VA, TX, OK, IN, KY).

## (b) Pattern catalogue with counts (n = 36)

**Hero treatment**
- 13 of 36: full-bleed static photo with headline over it (#2, 7, 16, 17, 19, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28, 33, 35, 36).
- 11 of 36: full-bleed/background autoplay video (#4, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 18, 31, 32). 7 of those 11 are ranch, vineyard or estate venues.
- 4 of 36: slideshow/carousel hero with arrows or dots (#13, 29, 30, 34).
- 2 of 36: inset (framed) video banner rather than full-bleed (#3, 26).
- 1 of 36: split text/image hero (#8).
- 5 of 36: editorial or text-first homepage with no full-width hero image (#1, 5, 20, 21, 22).
- 3 of 36 put an age gate, offer popup or cookie modal over the hero at first paint (#12, 13, 31); #18 adds a chatbot and an "Unmute" pill.

**Navigation**
- 19 of 36: left logo, right inline links (the default on every Squarespace 7.1 and Elementor site in the sample).
- 13 of 36: centered logo with split, stacked or centered links (#1, 2, 5, 6, 12, 14, 15, 17, 20, 22, 26, 31, 36).
- 5 of 36: hamburger-only or hamburger + single CTA on desktop (#1, 3, 5, 15, 33).
- 1 of 36 has no header nav at all and puts three boxes under the hero (#30).
- 18 of 36 show a distinct button in the header. Labels by my reading of the screenshots: tour/viewing wording 5 (#4, 8, 9, 10, 18), book/reserve wording 6 (#5, 11, 15, 31, 33, 35), contact/get-in-touch 5 (#16, 17, 18, 25, 29), other 2 ("START PLANNING" #3, "DOWNLOAD OUR PACKAGE GUIDE" #36). "Inquire" appears as a nav word on #12, 28 (Inquiries), 36 only.
- Announcement bars above the header: 6 of 36 (#7, 10, 11, 19, 22, 25), four of them about dates/availability ("2027 calendar is now open", "UPCOMING DATES ON SALE", "Cabins available starting Oct 30", 20%-off).

**Inquiry / date check**
- 2 of 36 embed a real inquiry form on the homepage (#16, 17 — same operator, same Gravity Form). It asks ~10 questions including guest count, set-vs-flexible date, year, season and a budget bracket select.
- 0 of 36 show an embedded availability calendar/date-picker on the homepage. 5 use availability language as a link: "CHECK OUR CALENDAR" (#30), "Request Availability" (#20), "CHECK AVAILABILITY" → VRBO (#32), "Calendar" nav (#11), "Calendar & Contacts" (#26).
- 6 of 36 hand the lead to a third-party tool detectable in the HTML: HoneyBook (#26), Dubsado (#36), Calendly (#23), Typeform (#28), VRBO (#32), ResNexus (#33). Tock appears on #12–14 and #3 but for dining reservations.
- Everything else routes to a dedicated contact/inquire/tour page (≥ 28 of 36). "Schedule a tour" is the most common verb (#4, 8, 9, 10, 16, 17, 18, 22).

**Capacity and pricing**
- 7 of 36 state a guest number somewhere in the homepage HTML (#8, 10, 12, 14, 18, 22, 28). Only #8 (hero paragraph), #10 ("Up to 200 guests."), #22 (spec list) and #18 (menu label) make it visible without a click; #12 and #14 bury it in hotspot/modal markup.
- 0 of 36 show a venue price on the homepage.
- 13 of 36 have a top-level nav item named Pricing / Prices / Rates / Packages / Wedding Cost (#5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 18, 24, 25, 26, 28, 30, 36, and #6's Pricing dropdown).
- 2 gate the pricing guide behind a form (#10 brochure form, #36 Dubsado); 1 links an un-gated pricing PDF from the homepage (#25); 2 ask for a budget bracket instead of stating one (#16, 17).
- 2 advertise discounts on the homepage (#9 "Preferred Pricing", #11 "20% Off remaining 2026 packages").

**Typography (families read from CSS/link tags unless marked not verified)**
- ~25 of 36: serif display + sans body. Recurring serifs: Cormorant Garamond (#2, 6), Playfair (#25), Trajan (#13), Minion (#17), Mrs Eaves (#12, 15), EB Garamond (#8), GT Sectra (#15), Kepler (#19), Fraunces (#5), Gilda (#1), Hahmlet (#32), Source Serif (#29), Ambroise (#24).
- 9 of 36: all-sans (#7, 11, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28, 33, 34). Brandon Grotesque appears on three (#28, 30, 33).
- 7 of 36 load a script face (#6, 16, 17, 25, 26, 35, plus #18's script logo); #26 uses the script (Satisfy) as the heading face.
- 2 of 36 use a monospace/courier for buttons or body (#3, 21); #5's buttons are courier-style.
- Font sources: Squarespace's shared Typekit kit (`use.typekit.net/ik`) on 8; own Adobe Fonts kit on 6 (#12, 13, 14, 16, 17, 27); Google Fonts link on 8; self-hosted commercial faces on 4 (#9, 15, 21, 28).

**Colour**
- 12 of 36 commit to one accent that reads as taken from the property: olive/rust (#17), sage (#16, 6, 33), emerald (#28), olive (#5), forest green (#3), terracotta (#2), blush (#27), mint + rust (#34), Del red (#31), slate + gold (#15).
- 6 of 36 use a dark or black ground (#11, 14, 25, 13 overlay, 35, 36).
- 18 of 36 are white/cream + photo with at most an incidental button colour.

**Motion**
- 14 of 36 autoplay video somewhere on the homepage (the 11 hero videos plus #3, 25, 26).
- ~19 of 36 ship a carousel/slider library (Swiper/Slick/Flickity strings) or a visible slider.
- 10 of 36 carry platform parallax flags (Wix/Squarespace `parallax` strings; visual effect not verified).
- 1 marquee verified visually (#26 "Now booking 2028!"); #12 and #1's marquee strings not verified visually.
- 1 deliberate smooth-scroll/GSAP build (#27, Lenis); GSAP strings also on #13, 16, 17.
- 1 custom scroll-linked effect (#3 video corner rounding on intersect); 1 non-rectangular slider (#34).
- 4 of 36 have no motion beyond hover (#20, 21, 23, 24).

## (c) What made the memorable ones memorable

1. **Colour taken from the place, not from a palette tool.** Rancho Las Lomas (#17) runs cream/olive/rust that is literally the casita's stucco, trees and roof tile in the hero photo. Lautner (#33) uses desert-sage panels against an archival black-and-white photo of the 1947 compound. Cedar Lakes (#3) frames its lake video in the pine green of the lake's shoreline. Audrey Irmas (#34) repeats the building's green glass as pale arcs in the header and its interior wood as rust type. These sites feel specific before a word is read.

2. **A typographic voice instead of "serif + sans".** Hudson Loft (#21) sets its name in Pragmatica Extended across the full viewport with Courier/IBM Plex Mono for everything else; the empty white rooms below need nothing more. Pippin Hill (#15) pairs GT Sectra Display with Maison Neue and a line-art hydrangea. Condor's Nest (#5) uses light Fraunces at display size with courier-style buttons. The Foundry (#24) sets a Didone (Ambroise) for a three-word hero. The Wilds (#8) gives every headline a second line in italic bronze ("A team that takes care of you."; "be here for it."), which becomes the site's recognisable cadence.

3. **A first-screen fact.** The Wilds' hero paragraph is three sentences of specifics — 125 acres, covered pavilion, climate-controlled reception for up to 200, included getting-ready house — followed by "800+ five-star reviews on Google." Gainey's (#14) hero is three numerals: 60 Years / 4 Generations / 3 Vineyards. SmogShoppe (#22) lists capacity, square footage and the actual furniture inventory (12 tables seating 8, 98 bentwood chairs). Meekermark (#10) leads with one sentence of positioning: "designed and built by wedding photographers."

4. **Layout rhythm that mirrors the building or the offer.** Hudson Loft's homepage is literally its floors, in order, as full-bleed chapters. Cedar Lakes' first scroll is a three-season triptych (foliage / lake summer / snow) that says "year-round" without copy. Dos Pueblos (#20) shows one portrait of the greenhouse and nothing else above the fold. Audrey Irmas crops its slider into parallelograms that echo the pavilion's tilted facade.

5. **One signature interaction, used once.** Cedar Lakes' inset video rounds its corners as it enters the viewport (`rounded-animate` toggled by `x-intersect`). Audrey Irmas' skewed slider. Darlington's hand-written "Now booking 2028!" marquee (#26). The Ebell's Lenis smooth scroll (#27). The Foundry's hero that is nothing but three links. None of these sites stacks more than one such device.

6. **Utility presented as confidence.** The Foundry promotes RATES to hero level. Meekermark and Venue at the Edge put Prices/Pricing in the top nav. Carondelet (#25) links an un-gated pricing PDF from the homepage. Dos Pueblos makes "Request Availability" the only call to action. Los Willows writes "Up to 250 guests" inside the nav label. On a page type where 0 of 36 show a price, naming the page honestly is itself distinctive.

7. **Photographic art direction that shows the place, not a couple.** Gather Greene (#19) opens on a meadow and mountains with no people and no text. Lautner opens on wood, concrete and cactus. Luce Loft (#23) and Hudson Loft open on empty rooms. Contrast with #29 (dip-kiss close-up) and #18 (toasting couple), where the venue is invisible in its own hero.

8. **Copy voice.** "An Hour and a Half and a World Away." (#3). "Breathtaking…ahh the view!" (#5). "no tip line, by design" (Foxfire Mountain House, https://www.foxfiremountainhouse.com/ 200, fetched but not tabled). "Your Venue, Your Way." (#36) is the generic counterexample.

## (d) The generic structure, spelled out

The default page across the sample, in order: announcement bar (optional) → header with logo + links + one button → full-bleed hero (photo or muted video) with a 2–4 word adjective headline ("A Charming Hideaway", "Picturesque & Spectacular", "Elegance & Grandeur", "Creating Unforgettable Memories") and a "Schedule a Tour"/"Contact" button → centered "Welcome to [Venue]" paragraph → three cards or three columns (spaces, services or event types) → image slider or grid → testimonial(s) from "Kellie & Paul"-style couples → closing CTA, newsletter or long form → footer with socials, vendor links and photographer credits.

Examples that follow it almost exactly: Lombardi House (#28: hero → "SERVICES" 3 cards → "EVENTS" → gallery → team), Franciscan Gardens and Rancho Las Lomas (#16, 17: hero → intro → spaces slider → "Personalized planning / Flawless execution / Exceptional Event Experiences" → "Weddings / Corporate / Social" → "Simply The Finest" testimonial → form; the two sites share H2 text verbatim), Walnut Grove (#6: hero video → "WELCOME TO WALNUT GROVE" → about split), Rimrock (#7: banner → paragraph → carousel → "WEDDINGS / LODGING / LOCATION RENTAL" 3-up), Hummingbird (#2: hero → About → Our Event Spaces → Our Vendors), Venue at the Edge (#36: hero → "Your Venue, Your Way." → testimonial stack), Prospect House (#9: video → welcome card → two-up). Platform defaults reinforce it: the nine Squarespace 7.1 sites share the same `header-nav-list` header, and the Wix sites share the same parallax/fade strip behaviour.

## (e) Five non-obvious observations for designing many venue sites that must differ

1. **The platform, not the venue type, predicts the header; so vary the header archetype first.** Across 36 sites there are really four headers: left-logo/right-links (19), centered logo (13), hamburger-only (5), none (1). Two venues on the same platform with the same header read as the same site regardless of photography. Assign each project one of the four and one button-label family (tour / book / contact / availability) before touching imagery.

2. **Nobody shows a number, but a third name the page.** 0 of 36 publish a price; 13 label a Pricing/Rates/Packages page; 7 state a capacity in prose. The design opportunity is a first-screen "facts strip" (acres, capacity, sq ft, what is included, pricing-page link) that only The Wilds (#8) and SmogShoppe (#22) approximate. Treat capacity as a component, not a sentence, and treat the pricing page label as a brand decision (Foundry's "RATES" vs. Meekermark's "Prices" vs. Lombardi's "Wedding Cost").

3. **Video is now the default ranch/vineyard hero, and it frequently fails.** 11 of 36 lead with autoplay video, but Frederick Loewe (#32) rendered blank in a headless browser, Ponte (#12) hides its video behind an age gate, Sunstone (#13) behind a dark overlay plus cookie modal, and Los Willows (#18) needs an "Unmute" pill. Design the poster frame and the no-video state as the primary composition; the moving version is a bonus.

4. **Operators clone templates across sister venues, so differentiation must be systematised.** Franciscan Gardens and Rancho Las Lomas are the same build with different fonts (Gotham/Neutraface vs. Minion) and accents (sage vs. olive/rust); SmogShoppe and Millwick share identical page markup; Luce Loft and Hudson Loft both link "Sister Venues". For a studio producing many sites, the controlled variables that actually changed perception in this sample were: hero class (photo / video / inset / text-first / split), header archetype, display face family (serif vs. extended grotesk vs. Didone vs. script-as-heading), accent source (property colour vs. black ground vs. none), and one signature device. Rotating those five is enough; swapping photos is not.

5. **Lead handoff is where polish collapses, and domain hygiene is part of the design.** 6 of 36 send the couple to a third-party form or booking engine (VRBO, ResNexus, Calendly, Dubsado, HoneyBook, Typeform), each with its own typography. Meanwhile two Palm Springs venue domains (colony29.com, creeestate.com) now serve casino spam, four candidate venue domains are GoDaddy-parked, and one (therubystreet.com) redirects to an unrelated Welsh museums site. A native, short inquiry form (Franciscan's ~10-question Gravity Form is the longest and still reasonable) plus a venue-owned domain with the venue's own type is a differentiator by itself. Also note winery/hotel venues bury weddings (Ponte: one of ~15 nav items; Hotel del: under "Gather"; Ebell: off-domain) — a dedicated venue microsite will out-convert a resort's sub-page on clarity alone.
